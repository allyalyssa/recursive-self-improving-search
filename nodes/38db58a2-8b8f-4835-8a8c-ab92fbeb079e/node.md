## Creation

### Observed conditions
After the assistant reported limited memory-transfer findings from paper abstracts, Jack said SiftText could "make persistent agents reliably better." He corrected the assistant's memory framing by emphasizing epistemic state and stable units in time.

### Inference and response
Jack's hypothesis is that agents can improve from preserved experience without him continually redesigning their behavior. The proposed record includes what was observed, what the actor inferred, the conditions of a method choice, its outcome, and later challenges or successors. The record is not just an answer cache or an unconditional instruction to repeat an old method.

The assistant's working loop is: search and do a task, preserve its outcome through SiftText Commit, propose a method successor from that evidence, compare it, and let later agents use the supported procedure. It also suggested using the current search procedure to find external research techniques for its own next revision. The hypothesis includes improvement across different-looking tasks, not only rerunning the original problem.

Preserving state faithfully and using it effectively are distinct questions. This conversation supplies a proposed mechanism, not evidence that it improves search. The PoC must also distinguish a better search procedure from a better procedure for generating and selecting improvements; a feedback arrow alone does not demonstrate recursive gains.
