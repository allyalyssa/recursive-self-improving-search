## Creation

### Observed conditions
The assistant proposed answer-history search, a self-healing agent, a context-budget comparison, monitoring, and fresh-agent continuation. Jack rejected these as insufficiently ambitious or insufficiently defined; he wanted something useful with research-level punch.

A later round offered A, failure inheritance; B, searching for evidence that would overturn a decision; C, self-improving search; and D, a local-model swarm. Jack initially liked B and C, rejected A as too close to familiar memory demonstrations, and said D was not search.

### Inference and response
The selection question became whether the project should improve a search answer or improve the search method itself.

## Completion
Jack chose C over B, then rejected presenting an ordinary practice/exam curve as the whole idea:

> "What people care about is recursive self-improvement. We gotta demo that live with SIFTEX feeding the recursion. Feeding the recursion, even if it's semi-manual."

He later proposed the title "recursive self-improving search" and its connection to SiftText's Eval program and product. B was superseded as this project's main direction, not empirically refuted. This node resolves the choice only; implementation and efficacy remain open.
